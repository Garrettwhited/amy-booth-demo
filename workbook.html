The Energy Mirror

A Simple, Practical Workbook for Women Who Keep Attracting Emotionally Unavailable Men
By Amy Lyn Bassett · Merge2Worlds

If you keep ending up with men who go quiet

If you keep ending up with men who go quiet, pull away, or can’t meet you where you are — it isn’t bad luck, and it isn’t because you are too much.

There is an energy underneath him. And there is an energy underneath you.

The men you attract are a mirror. What they reflect back is not your worth — it is your beliefs about relationships and love, and the inner child wounding you have been carrying since long before you met any of them. The good news: what is stored as energy can be released as energy — with love and grace, not shame.

The Energy Mirror shows you what the mirror is really reflecting, and gives you the practices to choose a different energy.

This workbook is for you if…

  • You keep attracting men who are emotionally unavailable, distant, or inconsistent

Start Here

This workbook is not about what he says.

Men who are emotionally unavailable can say beautiful words. They can also say almost nothing at all. The words change from man to man — the energy underneath is always the same. And that energy is what this workbook is about.

Here is the one idea everything in these pages is built on:

The energy a man carries reflects back to you the energy you are carrying inside yourself.

That is not blame. It is a mirror — and a mirror is good news, because a mirror shows you exactly where your own healing is. You cannot change his energy. You were never supposed to. But the moment your energy changes, the reflection changes — and a process like that, once it starts inside you, does not go backwards. You would not want it to.

How to use this workbook

  • Go in order. Each part builds on the one before it. • Ten honest minutes a day is enough. This is not homework; it is a practice. • Write by hand if you can. Energy moves when it gets out of your head and onto paper. • There are no wrong answers. There is only what is true.

What you will need: a pen, a quiet few minutes, and a willingness to look at the reflection without judging the woman in it.

Part 1 — The Energy Underneath Him

Forget his words for a moment. Forget the texts, the promises, the late-night talks. Instead, remember what it feels like to be near him.

An emotionally unavailable man carries a particular energy. In this work we call it wounded masculine energy — masculine energy that has gone into hiding or into armor. It usually feels like some mix of these:

  • Present in body, absent in energy. He is in the room, but nobody is home. His body sits next to you while his energy is somewhere else entirely. • Walled. There is a thickness between you and him that you can feel but never get through, no matter how gently you knock. • Withholding. Warmth comes in drops, never in a flow. Just enough to keep you reaching, never enough to rest in. • Taking without returning. The exchange runs one way. Your energy flows toward him; his does not flow back. • Heavy with ego, light on heart. Big on pride, control, being right — while the heart stays guarded and shut.

You do not need a man to confess any of this. Your body already knows it. Being near this energy usually feels like: reaching, waiting, tightening, hoping, going quiet inside, or trying harder.

Exercise — Feel it, don't analyze it

Think of the man (or the pattern of men) and answer from your body, not your head:

  1. When I am with him, my body feels: ______________________________________________
  2. When he pulls away, the feeling in me is: ________________________________________
  3. The word that best describes the energy between us is: _____________________________
  4. If his energy were weather, it would be: __________________________________________

Sit with what you wrote. That weather is real information. It is the climate you have been living in.

Part 2 — The Energy Underneath You

A mirror needs two sides. His energy found a match in yours — not because you are broken, but because somewhere in you, that energy felt like home.

Here is the shape it usually takes:

  • Your feminine energy is wide open and pouring out. Giving, soothing, understanding, fixing, waiting, hoping. The current runs strong — outward. • Your masculine energy — the part of you that protects you, holds your boundaries, and chooses you — has gone quiet. Nobody inside is standing guard. So your open heart has no one watching the door. • You lean. Energetically, you lean toward him — your attention, your mood, your plans bend around his energy. When he is warm, you bloom. When he is cold, you shrink.

This is what self-abandonment actually is, underneath the behavior: your energy leaves you, and goes to live in him.

Exercise — The Lean

Answer honestly:

  1. When he doesn't reach out, where does my energy go? _______________________________
  2. How much of my day is spent tuned to his mood, his distance, his next move? __________
  3. When did I last feel my energy fully in my own body, doing something just for me? ____
  4. If my energy had a guard at the door of my heart, what would that guard say? ___________

The truth to hold onto: You do not attract unavailable men because your love is wrong. You attract them because your giving energy is strong and your guarding energy is asleep — and his closed energy fits into that open, unguarded space like it was made for it. It was. It was made in childhood. That is Part 4. First, the mirror itself.

Part 3 — The Mirror: How His Energy Reflects Yours

This is the heart of the workbook. Read it slowly.

The universe you live in is built from the universe inside you. So the man in front of you is not random — he is a reflection of an energy you already carry. His unavailability is showing you, in living color, the places where you are unavailable to yourself.

The reflections usually look like this:

His energy — and what it reflects in you:

He is distant — The distance between you and yourself — how far you live from your own feelings and needs

He withholds — Where you withhold from yourself — rest, pleasure, kindness, your own dreams

He does not choose you — The places where you do not choose you — where everyone else’s needs outrank yours

His silence (or his outbursts) — Your inner voice — how you go silent on yourself, or turn on yourself, the same way

He takes and does not return — Your pattern of giving without receiving — in love, in money, in affection

He is armored and shut — The walls you built around your own heart to survive — the feelings you will not let yourself feel

Read the table again and notice which row stings. The sting is the map. That is your row. That is where the work is.

Exercise — Your Row

  1. The row that hit me hardest was: _________________________________________________
  2. A memory of this same energy from long before him: ________________________________
  3. What this reflection is trying to show me is: ________________________________________
  4. If I healed that one place in me, what would change — even if he never did? _____________

Write that last answer somewhere you will see it. That is the real subject of your healing. He was only ever the mirror. You are the face.

And here is the deepest layer of the reflection, the one the rest of this workbook works with: the face in the mirror is younger than you think. His energy does not mostly wound the grown woman — it lands on the wounded little girl inside her. His distance is the same distance she felt as a child. His silence rings in the same room of her heart where she waited, as a small girl, to be noticed, chosen, and held. What his energy reflects back is her inner child wounding — the old trauma, still alive in her energy, still running the pattern. That is why it hurts out of proportion to the moment. It is never only about this moment.

Part 4 — Where the Blueprint Was Printed

You learned the energy of love before you ever learned the words for it. You learned it at home, watching the two energies that raised you.

For so many women in this pattern, the first emotionally unavailable man was father — absent at work, or absent in the house: there in body, unreachable in energy. And beside him, mother — carrying loneliness, going silent or bursting out, teaching by example how a woman loves a man she cannot reach: by suffering quietly, by giving more, by abandoning herself.

That is the blueprint. From how your parents treated each other, you learned how to love, how to suffer, and how to abandon yourself. Then you grew up and called that energy "chemistry."

It is not chemistry. It is recognition. Your spirit is not chasing him. It is chasing the feeling of home — and trying, this time, to finally be chosen by it.

  5. The sentence about men/love I absorbed without anyone saying it: _____________________

Be gentle here. You are not blaming the people who raised you — most of them were living out a blueprint older than all of you, a pattern of masculine power misused and feminine energy unappreciated that runs through whole family lines. You are simply seeing the print, so you can stop copying it.

Part 5 — The Beliefs Underneath

Energy follows belief. What you believe about love decides what energy you will accept, chase, and call normal — long before any man walks in.

Most of those beliefs were never chosen. They were absorbed. A child absorbs how her home speaks about everything: how to communicate, how to speak about money, scarcity, and lack — and how to speak about love. She does not decide what is true. She breathes it in like air. Then she grows up and calls those beliefs "just how I am" — when many of them are false insecurities and borrowed truths that were never hers to begin with.

Part 6 — The Little Girl in the Mirror

When he goes quiet, notice how old you feel.

Most women in this pattern do not feel like grown women in that moment. They feel small. Anxious. Too much. Not enough. Desperate to fix it, terrified it is their fault, willing to do anything to make the warmth come back. That is not the woman talking. That is the inner child — the little girl who learned that love is far away, that she has to earn closeness, that her feelings are too big, and that the people she loves most cannot quite reach her.

His energy does not create that wound. It reflects it — it presses exactly where the old hurt already lives. This is why you cannot think your way out of this pattern with the grown woman's mind. The healing has to reach the little girl.

Exercise — Meet Her

  1. When he pulls away, how old do I feel? ____________________________________________
  2. What did that little girl need most that she did not get? ______________________________
  3. What did she decide about herself back then? (Examples: "I'm too much." "I have to be good to be loved." "My needs don't matter.") ________________________________________________
  4. What does she do now, in my adult relationships, to try to finally get it? ______________
  5. What would she need to hear — from me, today? ___________________________________

Practice — Talk to her directly (5 minutes)

Close your eyes. See her at the age you wrote above. In your mind, kneel down to her level and tell her the truth she never heard: You are not too much. It was never your job to earn love. I am here now, and I am not leaving you. Do this whenever the pattern gets loud. You are not being dramatic. You are reparenting the exact part of you the mirror keeps showing you.

Part 7 — Your Inner Voice Is an Energy Too

How your mother talked to you became how you talk to yourself. Her voice moved in and became your inner voice — and that voice is an energy you carry into every relationship.

So when a man goes silent, the silence outside meets a voice inside that already says: You're too much. You drove him away. Fix it. Be smaller. Try harder. His distance doesn't have to accuse you. Your own voice does it for him.

And when that voice gets loud, there are two old ways it comes out: you explode, or you stuff it down. Both are the wounded child's strategies. The practice that heals is the third way:

The Pause Practice

  1. Stop. When the voice starts — self-doubt, "you're too much," the urge to explode or to disappear — put both feet on the floor.
  2. Step back. Picture yourself stepping back from the voice, like stepping back from a painting to see the whole of it.
  3. Look at it. Ask: Whose voice is this, really? How old is the part of me that believes it?
  4. Answer it as the grown woman. Out loud if you can: That is an old voice. She is safe now. I choose what is true.

Part 8 — The Giving & Receiving Current

Energy moves between people like a current. In a healthy exchange, it flows both ways — giving and receiving in balance: love, attention, money, affection, care.

In this pattern, the current runs one way. You give. He receives — or doesn't even do that; he just lets it pool at his wall. And underneath, the reason is an old, false insecurity that was never even yours: the belief that you must pay for love in advance, in effort, in patience, in pieces of yourself.

Healing is not learning to give less love. It is learning to receive — to let the current flow back into you without guilt, deflection, or paying it back instantly.

Exercise — The Current Audit

Rate honestly, 1 (almost never) to 5 (almost always):

  • I give love/attention freely: ___ • I receive love/attention without discomfort: ___ • I give money/gifts/effort to keep connection: ___ • I can receive a gift, a compliment, or help without immediately returning it: ___ • I put myself first without feeling selfish: ___

Practice — Seal the leaks (this week)

Pick one leak and close it for seven days:

  • ☐ I will not text first to repair a silence that isn't mine to repair. • ☐ I will not explain, justify, or shrink my feelings to keep the peace. • ☐ I will receive one thing a day — a compliment, help, rest — and simply say thank you. • ☐ I will spend money/time on myself without earning it first.

Every leak you seal is energy that comes home to the little girl who needed it.

Part 9 — Balancing the Two Energies Within You

Here is the truth underneath all the twin flame teaching: the twin flame is already within you. It is the balancing of the feminine and masculine energy inside one person — you.

  • Your healthy inner masculine is the part of you that is steady, present, and protective. He holds your boundaries. He follows through. He chooses you, every day, and he stands guard so your heart can stay open safely. This is the energy your father could not give you — and the energy you kept hoping a man would bring from outside. • Your healthy inner feminine is the part of you that feels, receives, creates, and softens. She does not chase, because she knows her worth. She can rest, because someone inside is standing guard.

When your inner masculine wakes up, something changes outside: a closed-off man no longer feels like a challenge or a home. He just feels like what he is — unavailable. And an available man's steady, present energy starts to feel familiar instead of "boring," because now it matches something in you.

Exercise — The Daily Balance Check (2 minutes)

Each evening, ask:

  1. Today, did I protect myself? (my time, my no, my peace) _____________________________
  2. Today, did I let myself receive? (rest, care, pleasure, help) __________________________
  3. Which energy needs me tomorrow — the guard, or the soft place? ______________________

That is the whole twin flame journey, practiced in two minutes a day: the two energies in you, learning to love each other.

Part 10 — Shadow Work on the Reflection

Shadow work means looking at the parts of yourself you learned to hide — the feelings, needs, and wounds that got pushed into the dark — instead of stuffing them down or letting them explode out sideways.

And here is why it comes first: anyone starting a relationship should do this work beforehand, because without it, the unconscious part is running the show, and no relationship can begin on a solid foundation. The unhealed child will keep picking the same mirror.

The Reflection Worksheet — use it every time his energy (or the memory of it) hits you hard:

  1. What his energy did: (went silent for two days / pulled away after a close night / …)
  2. What lit up in me: (panic / shame / the feeling of being too much / …)
  3. The old wound it touched: (the little girl who … ) ______________________________
  4. What she needed then: _________________________________________________________
  5. What I give her now, myself: (a boundary, a rest, a kind word, a no, a held feeling)

Five rows of this worksheet will teach you more about your pattern than five years of analyzing him. He is not the study. The reflection is.

Five shadow questions — sit with one a day, and write what comes:

  1. What feeling do I judge most in myself — the one I call "too much" or "not enough"?
  2. What do I pretend not to need?
  3. Where do I perform being fine?
  4. What would I have to feel if I stopped chasing?
  5. What is the part of me I most want him to love — that I have never loved myself?

If what comes up in this work includes abuse, fear for your safety, or trauma that floods you and won't settle, this workbook is not the tool for that — please reach for a counselor or a support line in your area. Getting support is also shadow work. It is the inner masculine, protecting you.

Part 11 — The Emotional Body: Releasing Stored Energy with Love and Grace

Everything in this workbook comes down to one thing: energy.

The wounds, the beliefs, the trauma — none of it lives only in your thoughts. It lives in your emotional body: the stored energy of every feeling that never got to move. The tightness in your chest when he goes silent. The heaviness you carry into rooms. The knot that forms before you even know why. That is stored energy — old feeling, still living in you, still quietly running the pattern.

Hear this clearly: stored energy can be released. All of it. But it does not release by force. You cannot fight it out of yourself, shame it out, or hate yourself free of it — war is the same heavy energy that stored it in the first place.

It releases the way it was always meant to: felt fully, and met with love and grace. Grace means you stop punishing yourself for carrying it. Love means you thank it — it was protecting the little girl, the only way it knew how — and then you let it go.

Signs energy is moving: tears that come from nowhere, a deep sigh, yawning, warmth spreading, sudden lightness — or deep tiredness afterward, the way the body rests after putting down something heavy.

The Release Practice (about 10 minutes)

  1. Sit somewhere quiet. Put one hand on your heart, or on the place in your body that feels heaviest.
  2. Name what is stored there: the feeling, the memory, or the belief. Say it plainly: "This is the grief." "This is the waiting." "This is 'I'm too much.'"
  3. Feel it without the story — just the sensation itself. Breathe into that spot for about ninety seconds. Sensation, not analysis.
  4. Speak to it with love: "You kept her safe. Thank you. You don't have to hold this anymore."
  5. On every exhale, picture it leaving — like breath, like fog lifting, like light draining out of a heavy room.
  6. Fill the space on purpose. Energy hates a vacuum. Name what you place there instead: peace. choosing myself. softness. rest.
  7. Close with grace: "I release this with love. I keep the lesson. I let go of the weight."

Exercise — My Stored Energy Map

The rules of release

  • Release comes in layers. You are not doing it wrong if there is another layer next month. • Crying is release, not weakness. So is shaking, walking it out, dancing it loose, singing. Energy moves through the body — let the body help. • Rest after. Putting down years of weight is real work. • Never release with hatred toward yourself. Love and grace are not the reward at the end of this practice. They are the practice.

Part 12 — Communication: The Energy You Speak From

Conflict is never really about the words either. It is two energies meeting — and in this pattern, the meeting usually happens between his wall and your wound.

Look at the blueprint again: in the home most of us grew up in, hard feelings came out in exactly two ways — the outburst and the silent treatment. Explosion or disappearance. So that is what we learned communication is: when it hurts, you either blow up or you shut down. And we carry both into love — sometimes we are the one who goes silent and punishes; sometimes we are the one chasing a man who has.

Speaking from your new energy sounds different, because it is different:

  • Your inner masculine holds you steady: you can pause without abandoning the conversation. • Your inner feminine stays honest: you say the real feeling, not the armored version of it.

The most important distinction in this workbook: a wall and a pause look the same from outside and are opposites in energy. A wall says, "I'm done with you — come find me." A pause says, "I need twenty minutes to settle. I will come back at seven, and we will finish this." One is stored energy running the show. The other is resolution energy. Learn to offer pauses. Learn to recognize walls — his and yours.

Before any hard conversation, ask yourself three questions:

  1. What do I actually feel right now — underneath the anger?
  2. What does the little girl in me fear is about to happen?
  3. What do I need, in one honest sentence?

Resolution Questions — ask these of yourself first, then together:

For yourself:

  • What is this conflict really about, underneath the surface topic?
  • What old wound is awake in me right now?
  • What do I need in order to feel safe and close again?
  • Am I trying to solve the problem — or to protect the wound?

Together (each person answers all three):

  • "What did you hear me say?" (listen; do not correct yet)
  • "What did you feel when it happened?"
  • "What do you need from me right now — honestly?"

To close, together:

  • "What would resolution look like for both of us?"
  • "Can we agree the problem is the pattern — not each other?"

One last truth about communication: repair is not apology words. Repair is changed energy afterward — a current that actually flows differently in the days after the talk. Watch the energy, not the speech.

Part 13 — Intimacy: Letting Yourself Be Met

Intimacy is not bodies. Intimacy is energy closeness — being seen, felt, and received by another person, all the way in.

That is why this pattern can include plenty of physical closeness and almost no intimacy at all: close in body, miles away in energy. And it is why the cruelest moment is so often the morning after closeness — his energy retreats, because real intimacy asks for an open heart, and his heart is walled.

But check the mirror one more time. Many women in this pattern retreat too — picking a fight after a tender night, going numb, getting busy, finding the flaw. Because being truly met touches the wound directly: if he sees all of me, he will see that I am too much. The little girl would rather be chased than seen.

So intimacy work starts where everything in this workbook starts — with you not abandoning yourself in closeness. Staying in your body. Knowing your own feelings while they are happening. Letting yourself receive instead of performing, managing, or earning.

Exercise — Where Do I Close?

Check what is true:

  • ☐ When he gets close, I soften. • ☐ When he gets close, I tense, perform, or try to be "enough." • ☐ After closeness, I feel full and calm. • ☐ After closeness, I feel anxious, exposed, or start scanning for distance. • ☐ I can let myself be comforted without deflecting it. • ☐ Being fully seen feels dangerous to some part of me.

Reflection questions

  1. What am I afraid he would see if I let him all the way in? ___________________________
  2. When in my life have I felt most truly met — and what made it different? ____________
  3. What does receiving closeness feel like in my body — safe, or dangerous? _____________
  4. What would intimacy look like if the little girl in me wasn't afraid? _________________

Practice — Five Minutes of Being Met by Yourself

Once a day: sit down, hand on heart, and simply stay with whatever you feel for five minutes. No fixing, no analyzing, no phone. Just: "I'm here. I'm not leaving." It sounds too small to matter. It is not. A woman who can be met by herself stops begging walled hearts to do it for her — and starts recognizing the men who actually can.

Part 14 — Choosing a Different Energy

You will not choose differently by making better lists about men. You will choose differently when your energy changes — because then a different energy is what feels like home.

What available masculine energy feels like (learn its feel, not its words):

  • Steady instead of hot-and-cold — the same temperature on Tuesday as on Saturday • Present — his energy is in the room, in the conversation, in the plan • Warm without a wall — you can feel him receiving you, not just tolerating you • Giving flows back — the current moves toward you without being begged • Your body softens around him instead of tightening

The Body Test: After time with a man, ask one question: Is my body softer or tighter than before I saw him? Your nervous system has been reading energy your whole life. Start letting it vote.

Part 15 — For the Extra-Sensitive Woman: Awareness, Ancient Wisdom, and Source Energy

If you are neurodivergent — autistic, ADHD, sensory-processing different, or wired in another way that makes you take in more of the world — this part is especially for you. The same is true if you have always been the woman who feels a room before she understands it.

You may have been called too sensitive, too intense, too emotional, or too much. But sensitivity is not the wound. Sensitivity is a way of knowing. The wound is what happened when nobody knew how to honor that knowing — including, at times, you.

An extra-sensitive woman does not receive energy the way everyone else does. She may notice a change in tone, a pause before a reply, tension nobody has named, or the exact moment warmth disappears. Her body often knows about the wall before her mind finds words for it. That is why an emotionally unavailable man can affect her so deeply: it is not only heartbreak. Inconsistency can flood her emotional body, send her mind searching for certainty, and leave her spirit feeling cut off from itself.

Needing clarity is not neediness. For many neurodivergent women, clarity, consistency, and time to process are how the nervous system stays regulated. Mixed signals are not mysterious or exciting — they can become an extra burden the whole body has to carry.

The wisdom in your three energy bodies

In the language of this workbook, you are more than a physical body. Your emotional, mental, and spiritual bodies of energy each hold wisdom — knowledge older than this relationship, older than the pattern, and sometimes older than the words you have for it.

Your emotional body feels first. It holds lived and ancestral wisdom: grief that taught you something, empathy that recognizes another person's weather, and warning signals that arrive as sensation before they become thought. It may speak through tears, tightness, warmth, a sudden calm, or an unmistakable heaviness. Its wisdom asks: What am I feeling, and what is this feeling trying to show or protect?

Your mental body notices patterns. Many neurodivergent women think deeply, connect details others miss, and recognize a cycle long before they can prove it. This wisdom may arrive as a clear recognition: I have lived this pattern before. But the same powerful mind can loop when it is overwhelmed — replaying conversations, trying to solve silence, searching for the one explanation that will make the pain stop. Wisdom and fear can both use the mind. Wisdom is clear and settles the body. Fear repeats, tightens, accuses, and demands an answer right now.

Your spiritual body is your connection to Source energy — the living wisdom within and beyond you. It may speak through intuition, dreams, synchronicity, prayer, a deep inner yes or no, or a quiet peace that does not depend on circumstances. It does not usually shout over noise. It becomes easier to hear when you create quiet, regulate your senses, and stop abandoning yourself long enough to listen.

Your ancient wisdom is not one voice. It is the harmony of all three: the emotional body sensing, the mental body recognizing, and the spiritual body knowing.

Your sensitivity changes what you need

Check what is true for you:

  • ☐ I can feel another person's energy change before anything is said. • ☐ Inconsistent communication leaves me replaying, researching, or unable to settle. • ☐ Silence, rejection, or sudden distance can feel physically overwhelming — not just disappointing. • ☐ Noise, light, crowds, strong smells, or too much social time can use up my energy. • ☐ I need time alone after emotional or physical intimacy to return to myself. • ☐ I may mask — performing calm, easy, or "fine" — while my body is overloaded inside. • ☐ Direct, kind, predictable communication helps my whole system relax.

None of this makes you difficult to love. It tells the truth about how you are wired. A partner does not have to be perfect, but he does need the willingness to understand, communicate clearly, and repair when his actions affect you. Energy that constantly leaves you flooded, guessing, and recovering is not compatible with your well-being — no matter how strong the attraction feels.

Exercise — Meet Your Three Energy Bodies

Choose one relationship moment and listen through each body:

  1. Emotional body — What do I feel? Where do I feel it? What may this feeling be trying to show or protect?
  2. Mental body — What pattern do I clearly recognize? What is clear insight, and what is a fear loop?
  3. Spiritual body — When I become quiet, what simple truth do I already know?
  4. What do all three seem to be saying together?

Practice — Tune In to Source Energy

Give yourself about ten minutes, and make the space as sensory-friendly as you can: lower the light, silence the phone, choose a comfortable position, and allow movement, rocking, or stimming if that helps your body settle.

  1. Ground. Feel your feet, your back, or whatever is supporting you. Breathe a little more slowly. Name what you can see, hear, and feel.
  2. Arrive in your bodies. Place one hand on your heart and one on your belly, or wherever the emotion feels strongest. Silently say: I am here. I am listening.
  3. Ask one question. Keep it simple: Source, what do I most need to know about this connection? Or: What is my next right step?
  4. Listen in three channels. Notice a sensation or emotion, an image or pattern, and any quiet knowing that comes. Do not argue with the first impressions.
  5. Write them down. Capture the words, pictures, body sensations, or single sentence exactly as they came.
  6. Discern. Source wisdom may carry a hard truth, but it does not shame you, rush you, or call you too much. It feels clear, grounded, and self-respecting. If what you hear is panicked, looping, or self-hating, that is a wound asking for care — regulate first and listen again later.
  7. Close and integrate. Thank Source. Drink water. Move, rest, eat, or step outside. Receiving wisdom is only the first half; letting your body settle around it is the second.
